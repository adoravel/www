in 1964, Washington dispatched a naval task force toward Brazil under
[Operation Brother Sam] to back a military coup. it ushered in two decades of state
repression, torture, and censorship in brazil. but it didn't start or end with us: from
[overthrowing democratically elected governments in Iran (1953)] and [Guatemala (1954)],
to enabling [mass killings in Indonesia (1965)] and
[backing dictator Augusto Pinochet in Chile], american foreign policy has rewritten global
history in blood.

most americans do not even know these events happened.

living at the centre of an empire breeds a dangerous form of provincialism. people across
the global south are forced to learn the politics of core capitalist nations just to
anticipate how their choices might wreck our local economies or fund yet another
interventionist measure. meanwhile, their average citizen lives in total insulation from
the fallout their taxes fund across the globe. it is a bizarre asymmetry where the victims
carry the history while the perpetrator stays oblivious. ignorance becomes a luxury.

many people hyperbole the reality of media bias, acting as though the US is subject to
strict state-sponsored censorship or filtering every single international matter. that is
clearly not case. if anything, claiming «media filtering» is a lazy cop-out, because an
insular domestic news cycle or a sanitised high school curriculum explains where your
blind spots started, but it doesn't give you a lifelong pass to keep them forever. when
you have the entire internet in your hands and live in a developed nation, hiding behind
curated news channels is no longer an institutional barrier.

passivity is an active choice; having unlimited information at your fingertips whilst
claiming you don't know what to search is pure intellectual inertia. lack of initiative is
not an informational blockade.

resource scarcity in the global south does not prevent people from building awareness.
public education across impoverished nations faces chronic underfunding, broken
infrastructure, and political suppression. yet populations operating under those exact
material constraints routinely develop sharp consciousness because their lives depend on
understanding power.

remaining oblivious allows you to enjoy the material rewards of global dominance without
facing the moral invoice. the economic stability, strong currency, and cheap commodities
enjoyed in the Global North are directly tied to decades of military intervention and
forced market opens across the Global South. claiming you «didn't know» lets you consume
the profits of empire while disavowing its methods. you harvest the benefits of a history
you refuse to read.

an explanation of historical trauma is not an exemption from present-day accountability.
nobody carries personal blame for decades or centuries of exploitation before their birth,
but everybody inherits its structural legacy. once you become aware that your perspective
has been curated for your domestic comfort, correcting that deficit becomes a fundamental
ethical responsibility.

#### remaining comfortably unaware is a privilege afforded only to those whom the status quo protects.
