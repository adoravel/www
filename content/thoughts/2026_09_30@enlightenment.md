i am lowkey fed up with the way people don't get that the same insularity that erases
foreign intervention hollows out domestic social critique.

when structural awareness is missing, solidarity decomposes into pure aesthetic posturing.
people buy the vocabulary of liberation like the newest iPhone, learning what to endorse
without ever grasping the power dynamics underneath.

sitting outside an axis of harm does not make your ignorance neutral.

when someone isn't directly crushed by a specific system, studying inequality gets treated
as an optional hobby rather than a civic baseline. whether it's racial hierarchy, queer
survival, disability, or economic brutality, people view structural violence as a
localised tragedy that belongs strictly to its victims.

this shallow understanding reduces deep material struggles down to mere etiquette.

instead of analysing housing access, labour exploitation, or institutional leverage,
people flatten sociology into a game of rules. moral gatekeepers obsess over policing
vocabulary, treating a misused term as an irredeemable sin while ignoring the actual
material conditions of the people involved. context? reclamation? historical intent?
[yeah, no, just fuck all that rubbish].

language becomes the primary battlefield because managing words requires little to no
material sacrifice.

reactionaries then weaponise that rigid prescriptivism to whine about
<abbr title="which mind you, it's fucking bullshit">reverse discrimination.</abbr> they
act as if respecting a cultural boundary or watching an oppressed group reclaim a slur is
equivalent to systemic violence, collapsing centuries of historical trauma into a petty
debate over who gets access to what terms. this fixation on vocabulary allows people to
completely dodge the reality of inherited material privilege.

you do not inherit the personal moral guilt of your ancestors, but you inherit their
estate. the institutional safety, credit access, clean air, and generational stability
enjoyed by dominant demographics were paid for by historical exploitational. complaining
about losing unearned comfort is a total inversion of reality.
<span title="😭😭😭😭❓❓❓❓❓❓❓❓❓">it is claiming victimhood while continuing to
collect interest on stolen inheritance, if anything.</span>

<abbr title="in my i̶n̶d̶u̶b̶i̶t̶a̶b̶l̶e̶ opinion">imo</abbr>, this moral prescriptivism gets even
uglier when it turns inward against the very communities trying to survive. inside
marginalised spaces, you constantly see people turning on each other over the reclamation
of words like
<span title="ooOOoooOoooOo 👻👻 booooo the so-called ugly words, man :/">«faggot» or
«nigga»</span>, demanding blanket bans under the guise of harm reduction or any other
similar unfounded bullshit. often there isn't even a conscious strategic intent behind it.
it frequently boils down to basic personal discomfort.

wanting a word to vanish because it triggers personal trauma is completely understandable,
but turning individual discomfort into a universal law for your peers strips away
collective agency. to demand that a community cease recontextualising the very tools used
to dehumanise them flattens living, evolving history into some sort of fragile caricature.
it mistakes managing your own emotional boundaries for actual liberation, forcing others
to adjust their cultural expression just so you can be in your zone of comfort.

that all being said, <strong>until you actively seek out the material histories and
structural realities you were never taught, your progressivism is just a hobby and you're
nothing but a
<a href="https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/bugger">bugger</a> to any
proper movements.</strong>
